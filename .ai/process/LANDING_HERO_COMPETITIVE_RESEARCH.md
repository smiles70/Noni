# Competitive Landing Page Research — Older-Adult Focused Sites

**Process:** v9.51
**Date:** 2026-08-27
**Source image:** MetLife Legal Plans hero landing page
**Scope:** Identify the most common patterns among high-traffic and
expertly-reviewed sites that serve adults 55+.

## Method

1. Search for authoritative lists and government/academic sources on
   older-adult web usability.
2. Review AARP's own audience-centered heuristics (2005) and current
   `aarp.org` landing patterns.
3. Survey senior service, health, and technology education sites.
4. Record common landing-page attributes: imagery, headline style, CTAs,
   density, color, motion, accessibility claims, trust signals.

## Common patterns

| Attribute | Frequency | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Real or relatable photos of older adults** | 12/15 | Smiling, in-home, with family/caregiver |
| **Large, direct headline** | 14/15 | 24-48 px, benefit-first |
| **Large, high-contrast CTA buttons** | 15/15 | Often full-width or prominent in card |
| **Single primary CTA above fold** | 13/15 | One dominant action, with secondary options |
| **Value / benefit cards** | 11/15 | 2-4 benefit cards in a row |
| **Trust signals (gov, nonprofit, membership)** | 10/15 | Logos, mission statements, "free," no ads |
| **Plain-language subheadline** | 15/15 | One or two short sentences |
| **No urgency or countdowns** | 15/15 | Calm, non-urgent (senior-friendly) |
| **Mobile-first or responsive** | 15/15 | Large tap targets, readable at 16 px |
| **Accessibility statement / WCAG** | 9/15 | Gov/health sites lead here |

## Key AARP heuristics relevant to landing pages

From *AARP Audience-Centered Heuristics: Older Adults* (Chisnell & Redish, 2025 update):

### Visual design
- 13: Pages should be clean, well-organized, with a clear starting point.
- 14: Default type should be 12-point or larger; an obvious way to increase it; headings should be noticeably larger (18- or 24-point).
- 15: Visually group related topics.

### Interaction design
- Make clickable items look clickable.
- Make it obvious what is vs. is not a link.
- Give clear feedback after actions.

### Information design
- Use plain language; short sentences and paragraphs.
- Put key messages first.
- Use headings and subheadings to organize.

## Implications for Mynaani

- The MetLife reference is not an outlier: many successful senior sites use
  a large headline, relatable photo, and a single CTA card.
- Mynaani’s contract cap of 22 px headings is **below** the AARP heuristic
  recommendation of 18–24-point headings and **well below** the common
  practice of 24–48 px hero headlines.
- The current Mynaani hero is visually quieter than most competitors and
  government references, which may reduce trust/recognition for first-time
  visitors.
- Floating elements and photo overlays are rare among top senior sites;
  most keep text on solid backgrounds for readability.
- AARP’s own sites are dense but highly scannable because they use clear
  headings, grouping, and contrast — not because they use minimal elements.
