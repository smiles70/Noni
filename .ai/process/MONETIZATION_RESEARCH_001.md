# MONETIZATION-001: Can Mynaani be entirely free? Best-in-class model for older-adult AI learning

**Process:** v9.51  
**Intake:** `.ai/intake/2026-08-27-monetization-001.md`  
**Date:** 2026-08-27  
**Sources:** all external, published, and verifiable.

---

## 1. Bottom-line answer

**No, Mynaani cannot be *self-sustaining* as an entirely free product.**

For a **for-profit** older-adult AI learning product, the best-in-class model is not charitable grants or consumer subscriptions. It is a **B2B2C-first hybrid**:

- **Keep Modules 1-3 free to the learner.** They are the trust-building, digital-inclusion front door and the conversion funnel.
- **Offer Modules 4-5 as a one-time $39 purchase (or $59 caregiver gift)** for self-purchasers.
- **Sell bulk / sponsored access to employers, insurers, senior living, healthcare, and corporate wellness programs** so their members/employees use Mynaani at no cost.
- **Do not use ads or ad-supported freemium.** Ads contradict geragogy and erode the trust this audience requires.
- **Avoid recurring consumer subscriptions.** AARP 2025 and CHI 2024 evidence show this audience distrusts auto-renew and hard-to-cancel billing.

## 2. Evidence on "can it be fully free?"

### 2.1 Fully free models exist — but they require nonprofit / grant funding

| Source | Finding | Relevance |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| **Khan Academy Annual Report, SY24-25** | Khan Academy is free to users and generated **$117.5M** in revenue in FY24-25; **77% from contributions/grants**, ~$22M from program service (district partnerships). Major donors include Gates Foundation, Google, AT&T. | Free-at-the-point-of-use is possible only with a large donor/grant base and institutional partnerships. |
| **OATS / Senior Planet from AARP** | OATS is a charitable nonprofit. Senior Planet classes are free to learners. Funded by AARP, AT&T ($1M, 2025), JPMorganChase ($500k, 2025), T-Mobile, Google.org ($10M to AARP Foundation), Verizon, Humana Foundation. Reached 650,000+ participants in 2024. | Older-adult digital skills training is treated as a **social-impact / CSR category**, not a pure consumer market. |
| **TechReadySeniors** | Community-driven program that provides "free or low-cost training, devices, and ongoing tech support" funded by nonprofit contributions. | Confirms the sector norm: older-adult training is subsidized. |

### 2.2 Consumer WTP is low

| Source | Finding | Relevance |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| **AARP 2025 Tech Trends** | 60% of adults 50+ are **unwilling to pay a fee** for currently free digital services; 71% like a support service but expect it to be bundled or free. | Direct consumer monetization is capped. Most users expect free. |
| **Yamauchi et al., i-JMR 2017** | Median WTP for an elderly telecare service: **~$3.70/month**. | Even health-adjacent digital services command a very low monthly price ceiling. |
| **Yuan et al., RMHP 2024** | 70% of urban elderly not willing to pay for digital health tech; WTP tied to income. | Payment ability and willingness are not uniform; a one-size-fits-all price excludes many. |

### 2.3 Comparable consumer products in the older-adult / AI space charge, but adoption is lower

| Product | Model | Price | Notes |
|---------|-------|-------|-------|
| **ElderClass** | Per-course purchase | $320–$450/course | Targets adults 60+; high per-course price suggests low volume. |
| **RendeverLive** | Freemium subscription | $0–$99/month | Virtual community for seniors; free tier limited to 5 session credits. |
| **Ask Proxy** | Freemium subscription | 15 free texts, then $29/month | AI assistant for seniors via SMS; usage-based free tier. |
| **Senior Shield** | Freemium subscription / enterprise | $6.99/mo or $59.99/yr; enterprise custom | Scam safety for seniors; 7-day trial. |
| **Lumosity** | Freemium subscription | $11.99/mo; $59.99/yr; $299.95 lifetime | Brain training; free tier limited to 3 games/day. |
| **Duolingo** | Freemium with ads + subscription | $13.99/mo; $95.99/yr; $167.99/yr Max | Free-to-paid conversion 2-4% for language; older adults are not the core audience. |
| **LearningGPT.ai/seniors** | First 6 lessons free, then subscription | Not stated | AI literacy for seniors; free-first, then paywall. |

### 2.4 B2B2C and institutional funding is the dominant best-in-class path

| Source | Finding | Relevance |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| **GetSetUp / Stanford GSB case, 2026** | 4M registered users, 32 state government contracts, healthcare contracts. **80% government, 20% healthcare payers**. Near breakeven. Roughly 40M target users on fixed incomes. Direct consumer revenue is not the primary model. | The most comparable successful platform in the space relies on **institutional payers**, not consumer payments. |
| **GetSetUp / Inc42, 2021** | Subscription INR 300/mo or 3,000/yr, plus B2B2C via Tivity Health for free access. | Even with a consumer price, institutional licensing is the growth engine. |
| **OATS / Senior Planet licensing** | 450+ licensed partner sites across 35 states. Corporate sponsors (AT&T, T-Mobile, Verizon) fund free programming. | Distribution and funding come through institutions and CSR. |
| **AARP Foundation / Google.org** | $10M grant to train 25,000 low-income adults 50+. | Demonstrates that the funder is a corporation or foundation, not the learner. |
| **EY report, 2026** | Older adults need AI literacy; policymakers, educators, businesses, and community organizations should provide it. | Framing: AI literacy is a **shared responsibility**, not a consumer purchase. |

### 2.5 Edtech conversion benchmarks

| Source | Finding | Relevance |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| **EntrepreneurBible** | Language learning free-to-paid: 2-4%; test prep 8-15%; skill/vocational 5-10%; K-12 1-3%. | Older-adult AI literacy is closer to "general learning" than test prep, so expect the lower end. |
| **Lifecycle Architect, 2026** | No-CC free-trial median 12-20%; freemium paid upgrade 2-6%. | Freemium conversion is low; Mynaani's one-time purchase may convert better because the value is concrete. |
| **Duolingo Q4FY25 shareholder letter** | Duolingo is intentionally **investing $50M+ in the free user experience** at the cost of monetization, prioritizing word-of-mouth. | Even a scaled public company cannot monetize an older/lower-WTP audience directly. |

## 3. Force-ranked monetization approaches for Mynaani

Scoring: 1 (poor) to 5 (excellent) across **Sustainability**, **Learner Accessibility**, **Geragogy/Trust**, **Mynaani Fit**, **Operational Simplicity**, **Revenue Potential**.

| Rank | Approach | Sustainability | Accessibility | Geragogy | Fit | Simplicity | Revenue | Total |
|------|----------|---------------|--------------|----------|-----|------------|---------|-------|
| **1** | **Hybrid: free core + one-time paid + B2B2C sponsorship** | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 29 |
| **2** | **B2B2C / institutional licensing (GetSetUp model)** | 5 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 5 | 25 |
| **3** | **One-time paid, no free upper tier (current ADR 0021 variant)** | 3 | 2 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 21 |
| **4** | **Fully free, donor/grant funded (Khan Academy / OATS)** | 2 | 5 | 5 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 19 |
| **5** | **Freemium subscription, no ads (Lumosity / Ask Proxy)** | 3 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 18 |
| **6** | **Freemium subscription with ads (Duolingo-style)** | 3 | 3 | 1 | 2 | 4 | 3 | 16 |
| **7** | **Per-course purchase (ElderClass-style)** | 3 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 16 |
| **8** | **Usage-based / message-based (Ask Proxy SMS model)** | 3 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 16 |
| **9** | **Pure paid-only (no free tier)** | 2 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 3 | 2 | 11 |

### Force-rank notes

1. **Hybrid** is the clear winner. It mirrors GetSetUp, OATS, and Khan Academy: free core for trust and inclusion, one-time paid for those who can pay and want advanced features, and institutional/corporate contracts to cover the gap.
2. **B2B2C** is strong on sustainability and revenue but requires a separate sales motion.
3. **One-time paid only** is simpler but excludes the 60% unwilling-to-pay and conflicts with the AARP evidence.
4. **Fully free** is the most accessible and trustworthy but requires a nonprofit/grant funding machine Mynaani does not currently have.
5. **Freemium subscriptions** score low on geragogy because older adults specifically distrust auto-renew and hard-to-cancel patterns.
6. **Ads** score lowest on geragogy: ads are distracting, can be deceptive, and erode the calm, trust-first design.

## 4. Recommended Mynaani model

### 4.1 Keep the current free tier (Modules 1-3)

- **Why:** AARP evidence says older adults will not pay for something they perceive as currently free. Modules 1-3 are a complete, dignified AI literacy education on their own. They build trust before any payment.
- **Evidence:** OATS, Khan Academy, and GetSetUp all lead with free or very low-cost access to the front door.

### 4.2 Keep the one-time purchase for Modules 4-5

- **Why:** Avoids the subscription dark patterns this audience distrusts. $39 is below the annual cost of comparable subscriptions and converts to ~$3.25/mo over a year, near the telecare WTP anchor. $59 gift opens a higher-WTP caregiver channel.
- **Evidence:** CHI 2024 found hidden costs and hard-to-cancel are the top concerns for older adults. One-time purchase is the opposite of those patterns.

### 4.3 Add a B2B2C / institutional contract capability

This is the **new recommendation** and the best-in-class addition for a for-profit model.

- **B2B2C bulk licenses:** Employers, insurers, senior living operators, healthcare providers, and corporate wellness programs purchase bulk or per-seat licenses and offer Mynaani to their members/employees at no cost.
- **Sponsored individual codes:** A learner who cannot or will not pay receives an access code from their sponsoring organization. Mynaani is paid by the organization, not the learner.
- **Caregiver / family gifting:** Already in place ($59 gift) for direct consumer purchase.
- **Implementation:** A new `sponsor_purchase` or `access_code` flow. An organization buys N seats at a discounted per-seat rate (e.g., $25–$35). Each learner enters a code at the paywall and receives the same `modules_4_5` entitlement.

### 4.4 Do NOT

- Add ads or ad-supported freemium.
- Move to a subscription model for the core paid tier.
- Remove the free tier.
- Introduce urgency, countdowns, or scarcity messaging.

## 7. For-profit constraint (amendment)

Mynaani is a for-profit entity. This eliminates the donor/grant-funded path as the primary business model and makes institutional **contracts**, not **donations**, the best-in-class revenue engine.

### 7.1 Implications

- **No reliance on charitable contributions.** Khan Academy's 77% contribution model and OATS's grant-funded model are not directly applicable.
- **B2B2C contracts become the core revenue.** The payer is an organization (employer, insurer, senior living, healthcare provider, corporate wellness program) that pays Mynaani to offer the curriculum to its members/employees/customers for free.
- **Consumer one-time purchase becomes a profitable upsell, not the main engine.** Direct consumer revenue is capped by the AARP 60% unwilling-to-pay data; it is best treated as a high-margin, low-friction option for self-purchasers and caregivers.
- **Sponsorships must be structured as sponsorship / B2B contracts** (e.g., CSR marketing, benefit fulfillment, member acquisition) rather than tax-deductible charitable gifts.

### 7.2 Revised for-profit recommendation

| Tier | Payer | Learner cost | Mynaani revenue source |
|------|-------|--------------|----------------------|
| **Free core (Modules 1-3)** | Mynaani (cost of acquisition) | Free | N/A — trust building, conversion funnel |
| **Advanced (Modules 4-5)** | Learner / caregiver | $39 / $59 gift | Direct one-time purchase |
| **Sponsored / bulk access** | Employer, insurer, senior living, healthcare, corporate wellness | Free to learner | B2B contract / per-seat or flat fee |
| **Institutional license** | Senior center, library, community org | Free/discounted to members | Bulk license fee |

### 7.3 For-profit force-rank adjustment

For a for-profit entity, the top three models are:

1. **B2B2C / institutional contracts** — highest revenue, sustainable, scales to fixed-income audience.
2. **Hybrid: free core + one-time paid + B2B2C** — still the best overall, with B2B as the dominant revenue line.
3. **One-time paid only** — viable but limited by low consumer WTP.

### 7.4 Key open questions for a for-profit path

1. What is the B2B2C per-seat price and minimum contract size?
2. Which vertical has the fastest sales cycle: employer wellness, Medicare Advantage, senior living, or healthcare?
3. Does the one-time $39 consumer price need to rise to offset the cost of free-tier users?
4. What unit economics (Claude API cost per user, support cost) make the B2B2C price defensible?
