## Claude Is Not an Authority

Reinforcing that Claude offers suggestions, not answers.

#### Where Module 2 left you

Module 2 ended with a quiet rule: you decide how Claude fits into your life.

Module 3 is the protective module. It exists to keep that rule strong, even after years of using AI.

#### A confident voice is not a correct voice

Claude is not a doctor, a lawyer, a teacher, or a friend.

Claude is a tool that produces words. The words can be useful. They are not the truth itself.

You are the one who knows your life. That has not changed and will not change.

**Key idea:** Claude can sound sure even when it is wrong. Sounding sure is not the same as being right.

#### A confident-sounding wrong answer

Here is what "confident but wrong" can look like.

*Example — You ask Claude what year a book was published. Claude answers cleanly, with a single year and no hedge.*

Claude might say: “That book came out in 1962.”

Takeaway: The answer might be right. It might also be off by ten years. The clean voice is not proof. A library catalog or the cover of the book itself is the right place to settle it.

#### Who is the authority on what is right for you?

Read both. Pick the one that fits the rule from the principle page.

*Practice question:* Who is the authority on what is right for you?

- You are. Claude is one voice you may consult, not the deciding one.
- Claude is. It speaks with confidence, so it must know best.

> Correct answer (do not read verbatim — guide the caller): You are. Claude is one voice you may consult, not the deciding one.. Why: Claude is a tool. The authority over your life is, and stays, you.

## Keeping Your Judgment Sharp

Maintaining personal decision-making alongside AI use.

#### What we just settled

You learned that Claude can sound sure even when it is wrong.

This lesson is the next step: keeping your own thinking strong by actually using it.

#### Skills stay sharp by being used

If Claude does the thinking every time, your own thinking gets quieter.

Sometimes that is fine. Sometimes it is the difference between a habit and a crutch.

Doing a small task yourself - a list, a reply, a calculation - is a kind of exercise.

**Key idea:** Using your own judgment keeps your judgment strong.

#### A small task done two ways

Here is the same task handled two ways.

*Example — You need to add up the cost of three small items at the store. You could ask Claude. You could also do the sum yourself.*

Claude might say: “I can add those up for you. But you can also: take a breath, add them in your head, and trust the answer you get.”

Takeaway: Some small tasks are worth doing yourself. Each one is a quiet way of telling your mind it is still in charge.

#### Which approach keeps your skills sharp?

Read both. Pick the one that matches the rule from this lesson.

*Practice question:* A small task could be done by you or by Claude. Which keeps your skills sharper?

- Doing the small task yourself, when you have the time.
- Asking Claude to do it every time, even the small ones.

> Correct answer (do not read verbatim — guide the caller): Doing the small task yourself, when you have the time.. Why: Skills stay sharp by being used. Small tasks are good practice.

## Knowing When to Pause or Step Away

Recognizing when not using Claude is a healthy choice.

#### The thread so far

You learned that using your own judgment keeps it strong.

This lesson is the second half of that idea: sometimes the best move is to use Claude less, or not at all.

#### Stepping away is a strength

Some afternoons, the right move is to put the screen down.

Some questions deserve quiet, or a person, or a walk.

Putting Claude aside for an hour, a day, or a week takes nothing away from what you have learned.

**Key idea:** Choosing not to use Claude is a sign of strength, not failure.

#### A quiet afternoon without Claude

Here is what "stepping away" can look like.

*Example — It is Sunday afternoon. You had thought about asking Claude to help plan the week, but you also feel like reading on the porch.*

Claude might say: “(Nothing. The page stays closed.)”

Takeaway: The afternoon was yours. Claude will be there next time you want it.

#### Pausing — weakness or strength?

Read both. Pick the one that matches the rule from this lesson.

*Practice question:* You decide not to use Claude for a week. What is true of that choice?

- It is a sign of strength: you set the pace, not the tool.
- It is a sign of failure: you should have kept practicing.

> Correct answer (do not read verbatim — guide the caller): It is a sign of strength: you set the pace, not the tool.. Why: Stepping away is part of using a tool well. Nothing is lost.

## You Remain the Decision-Maker

Consolidating autonomy, confidence, and dignity in AI use.

#### What the whole course was about

You started this course not knowing what Claude was. You end it knowing more than most people about how to use AI well.

This last lesson is short. It is the rule that holds the rest together.

#### You remain the decision-maker

Through every lesson, the rule has been the same: Claude offers, you decide.

That rule does not change with practice or with time. It deepens.

You are capable, you are thoughtful, and you are in charge of your own life.

**Key idea:** Claude is a tool. You are the person. The decisions are yours.

#### The shape of a healthy relationship with a tool

Here is what the course is asking you to carry forward.

*Example — Six months from now, you are deciding whether to use Claude for something small or to handle it yourself. You think about it for a moment.*

Claude might say: “(Nothing — Claude is not part of this moment yet, and may not need to be.)”

Takeaway: The pause itself is the point. You thought before you reached. That is what the whole course was for.

#### Who decides?

Read both. Pick the one that matches the rule that ties this course together.

*Practice question:* At the end of the course, who decides how Claude fits into your life?

- You do. That has been true the whole time.
- Claude does, by being available whenever you open the page.

> Correct answer (do not read verbatim — guide the caller): You do. That has been true the whole time.. Why: You are the decision-maker. The course exists to keep that true.
