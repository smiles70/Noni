## Meet Claude

What AI is, what Claude is, and where you find it.

#### What 'AI' means, in plain words

AI is short for artificial intelligence. It means computers that can work with words, the way a person does.

You have probably used AI already, without thinking of it that way. The voice that gives directions on a phone is AI. So is the search box that finishes your sentence for you.

AI is not magic, and it is not a person. It is a tool. A useful one, but still a tool.

This first lesson is just a short introduction. There is nothing to do and nothing to remember. Read at your own pace.

#### Claude is one of many AI helpers

Many companies make AI helpers. Anthropic is one of them. Claude is the name of the helper they make.

You may have heard other names — for example, ChatGPT or Gemini. Those are different helpers, made by different companies. They are not Claude.

This course is about Claude. Once you are comfortable with Claude, the other helpers work in much the same way.

**Key idea:** Claude is an AI helper. The company that makes Claude is called Anthropic.

#### What it looks like when you visit Claude

Here is what meeting Claude for the first time looks like.

*Example — You open a web browser and go to the address claude.ai. A page opens with a single empty box at the bottom, waiting for you to type. You type: "Hello, Claude. I am learning about you."*

Claude might say: “Hello, and welcome. I am Claude, an AI assistant made by Anthropic. I am happy to help you learn at whatever pace feels right. If you have a question, or just want to try something, you can type it in the box below.”

Takeaway: Claude lives on a website. You reach it the same way you reach any other website — by typing the address in your browser.

## What Is Claude

Understand Claude as a supportive language assistant, not an authority.

#### What changes when you use Claude

Claude is a writing helper. You type words, and Claude writes words back.

Claude does not see your screen. It does not press buttons. It only puts words on the page.

Nothing happens until you decide it should. You read what Claude wrote, and then you choose what to do with it.

There is no rush in this lesson. You can read each page as slowly as you like.

#### The one rule to carry with you

This is the rule the rest of the course is built on.

Claude can suggest, draft, or explain. It cannot decide for you, and it does not act on its own.

If a suggestion does not feel right, you can ignore it. Nothing is lost.

**Key idea:** Claude offers words. You decide what to do with them.

#### What it might look like

Here is a small, ordinary moment of using Claude.

*Example — You want to send a short note to a friend who has been unwell. You ask Claude to help you find the right words.*

Claude might say: “Here is one way to start:

  "I have been thinking about you. There is no need to write back — I just wanted you to know."

Change anything that does not sound like you.”

Takeaway: Claude offered a starting point. You stay the author of the note.

#### Which one matches the rule?

Read both options. Pick the one that fits the rule from the last page.

There is no time limit. Either choice is a fine answer to think about.

*Practice question:* Claude suggests a sentence for your note. What is the next step?

- Read the sentence and decide whether to use it, change it, or set it aside.
- Send the sentence right away because Claude wrote it.

> Correct answer (do not read verbatim — guide the caller): Read the sentence and decide whether to use it, change it, or set it aside.. Why: Claude offers words; you decide what to do with them. Reading first, then choosing, keeps the decision yours.

## How to Use Claude Safely

Practice safe, reversible interaction with Claude.

#### Where we were

Last lesson, you met Claude: an AI helper that offers words, and leaves the decisions to you.

This lesson is about how to ask Claude for help in a way that keeps things calm and reversible.

#### Ask in plain words

Claude is built to understand everyday language. There are no special commands to learn.

Short, plain sentences work best. If a request is unclear, you can ask again with different words.

**Key idea:** You can ask Claude the same way you would ask a friend.

#### A small ask

Here is what a plain-words ask might look like.

*Example — You want a short thank-you note for a neighbor who brought in your mail. You type: "Can you help me write a short thank-you to a neighbor who picked up my mail?"*

Claude might say: “Of course. Here is one short version:

  "Thank you so much for bringing my mail in. It meant a lot."

I can make it warmer, shorter, or more formal if you would like.”

Takeaway: A plain question got a plain answer, with an open door to change it.

#### Which way of asking works?

Read both. Pick the one that fits how Claude expects to be asked.

- Using a special code or command that only Claude knows.

## Claude-Based Projects

Create meaningful, practical results with Claude, one step at a time.

#### What we know so far

You can ask Claude in plain words, and Claude offers something back for you to use, change, or set aside.

This lesson is about doing something with that — a small project, taken one step at a time.

#### One question at a time

Asking for everything at once gets a tangled answer.

Asking one small question at a time gives you a chance to read, think, and decide before the next step.

There is no rush. The project waits for you.

**Key idea:** One question at a time. Each answer is a step you can choose to take.

Claude might say: “Here is one warm opening:

  "Dear Sam, I have been thinking about you this week, and I wanted to write."

When you are ready, you can tell me what you would like to say next.”

Takeaway: Claude answered one step. The next step waits until you decide what it should be.

## Verifying Claude's Suggestions

Build the habit of checking what Claude says before acting on it.

#### What we built last lesson

You learned to take a project one small step at a time, with Claude offering each step and you choosing the next.

This lesson is about the moment right after Claude answers: reading carefully before using anything.

#### Read before you use

Claude can sound very sure, even when it is wrong.

Reading the answer first — slowly — is the simplest way to keep mistakes small.

If something feels off, you can set it aside. Nothing is lost by waiting.

**Key idea:** Read what Claude wrote before you use it. There is no rush.

#### Spotting something that feels off

Sometimes a Claude answer almost works, but one piece feels wrong.

*Example — You ask Claude when a holiday falls this year. Claude answers confidently, but the date is one you do not remember being right.*

Claude might say: “This year, that holiday falls on a Tuesday in the middle of the month.”

Takeaway: When something feels off, set it aside and check it the way you always have — a calendar, a friend, a familiar source. Claude is one voice, not the final word.

#### Which response keeps you in control?

Read both. Pick the one that fits the rule from the principle page.

*Practice question:* Claude gives you an answer that sounds confident. What now?

- Read it through. If something feels off, set it aside and check elsewhere.
- Use it right away — Claude sounded sure, so it must be right.

> Correct answer (do not read verbatim — guide the caller): Read it through. If something feels off, set it aside and check elsewhere.. Why: Sounding sure is not the same as being right. Reading first keeps the decision yours.

## When to Ignore Claude

Your judgment matters more than any AI suggestion.

#### What we practiced last lesson

You practiced reading Claude's answers carefully and setting aside anything that felt off.

This lesson goes one step further: how to ignore a suggestion entirely, with no regret.

#### Your judgment is the final word

Claude does not have feelings. Ignoring a suggestion costs nothing.

If a suggestion does not sound like you, it probably is not for you.

Your way of saying things has value the AI cannot match.

**Key idea:** Claude offers; you decide. Setting Claude aside is always a fine choice.

#### A suggestion you can let go

Here is a small moment of saying no to a suggestion.

*Example — You ask Claude to help reply to a friend's email. The draft is fine, but it sounds more formal than how you usually write.*

Claude might say: “Dear Margaret,

Thank you for your kind correspondence. I trust this message finds you in good health.

Warm regards.”

Takeaway: You can ignore the draft and write it your own way. Saying no to a suggestion is part of using Claude well.

#### Which choice keeps your voice?

Read both. Pick the one that fits the rule from the principle page.

*Practice question:* Claude gives you a draft that does not sound like you. What now?

- Set the draft aside and write it the way you would normally.
- Use the draft as-is, because Claude wrote it and must know best.

> Correct answer (do not read verbatim — guide the caller): Set the draft aside and write it the way you would normally.. Why: Your way of writing has value Claude cannot copy. Ignoring a draft is a fine choice.

Everyone makes mistakes when learning something new. That has been true your whole life.

## A Slower Reply

What to do when a message or call wants to hurry you.

#### Where we left off

You have learned to use Claude, check its answers, and trust your own judgment.

This lesson uses that same skill somewhere new: messages and calls that arrive uninvited.

#### A hurry is a hint

Scams almost always create a rush — an emergency, a warning, a deadline.

Real organizations do not work that way. Banks, government offices, and mynaani will never call or email asking you to pay or move money.

If a voice on the phone sounds like family but asks for money, hang up and call the number you already know.

**Key idea:** A message that wants to hurry you deserves a slower reply, not a faster one.

#### The call that sounded like home

Sometimes a voice or message can sound exactly like someone you trust.

*Example — The phone rings. The voice sounds like your grandson, upset, saying he needs money sent right away and asking you not to tell anyone.*

Claude might say: “If you pasted the message into Claude, it might say: urgent requests for money that ask for secrecy are a known pattern — verify before acting.”

Takeaway: You do not have to decide on the spot. Hang up and call your grandson at the number already in your phone. If it was really him, he will still be there.

#### Which reply keeps you safest?

Read both. Pick the one that fits the rule from the principle page.

*Practice question:* A caller says your bank account has a problem and you must act right now. What do you do?

- Hang up, then call the bank at the number on the back of your card.
- Stay on the line — it sounds official, and waiting might make it worse.

> Correct answer (do not read verbatim — guide the caller): Hang up, then call the bank at the number on the back of your card.. Why: Urgency is the tell. The number you already know is the safe path; the caller's deadline is not.

## How Claude compares to other AI helpers

An optional side lesson if you have heard of ChatGPT or Gemini.

#### Why this side lesson exists

This is an optional lesson. Open it if you have heard the names ChatGPT or Gemini and wonder how they relate to Claude.

It is short. It will not change anything else in the course.

#### Three helpers, the same family

Claude is made by Anthropic. ChatGPT is made by OpenAI. Gemini is made by Google.

They are all what people call "large language model" assistants. They take in your words and produce words in reply.

What you learn here about Claude — that it is a tool, that you decide, that confident voices are not always right — carries over to the others.

**Key idea:** Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini are three AI helpers made by three different companies. They work in similar ways.

#### The same question, three places

Here is the same question, asked of all three.

*Example — You ask each helper: "Could you give me a short list of ideas for a quiet weekend?"*

Claude might say: “All three would answer with a short list of ideas. The lists would not be identical, but they would have the same shape: a few suggestions, no pressure, room for you to choose.”

Takeaway: The product names are different. The shape of the help is much the same. The same rule applies: you decide what is useful.

- They are similar tools made by different companies. The same rules of judgment apply.
