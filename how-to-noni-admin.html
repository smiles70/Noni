1. Signing in and finding your way around

1
Open https://www.mynaani.com/admin. You will see the mynaani logo with Username and Password fields and a Log in button.
2
Enter your staff username and password, then Log in. Usernames are not case-sensitive.
3
The console opens on the Overview screen. You are signed in for up to 8 hours. Use Sign out (top right) when you finish — especially on a shared computer.

The console is organized by the jobs you do

Down the left side:

  • Overview — the landing screen: what needs attention
  • Organizations — find and open organizations; onboard new ones
  • Accounts — look up a learner's account (support)
  • Flags — sharing signals waiting for review
  • Audit — every staff change, who made it, and when
This login is separate from the learner sign-in — employees only. No magic link, no Stripe, nothing to do with learner accounts.
“That username or password didn't match” — check spelling. After 5 tries in a minute, sign-in pauses for a minute. That is a security brake, not a bug.

2. The Overview — your landing screen

  • Needs attention — four cards: organizations on file, seats used across all licenses, pending flags, licenses expiring within 30 days.
  • + New organization — the big button that starts onboarding (section 3).
  • Expiring soon — orgs whose licenses end within 30 days; click a name to open it.
  • Recent activity — the last staff changes (who, what, which org) straight from the audit log.

3. Onboarding a company or community

Everything is done in one guided form — + New organization.

1
Open the wizard. From Overview or Organizations, click + New organization.
2
Details. Name, type (nonprofit/community, for-profit or senior living, health plan), tier, and community size.
3
Location. Street, city, state, ZIP, phone — the facility's business address.
4
Contacts. Add one or more named contacts (name, email, phone) and tick primary on the person you send codes to. Primary contact email at the bottom is required.
5
License & go-live. Seats, license end date, and the slug (e.g. oaktown-seniors → mynaani.com/for/oaktown-seniors). Click Create organization — the console creates the org, attaches the license, generates one access code per seat, and sets the public page in order.
6
Copy the results. The success screen shows the redemption link and the full code list. Copy them now — send the link and one code per learner to the primary contact.
7
Learners redeem. Each learner opens the link, signs in with their own email, enters their code, and the seat is theirs. Watch seats used fill on the org's page.
If onboarding stops partway, the message says so — the organization may already exist. Check Organizations before retrying so you don't create a duplicate.

4. Managing seats and licenses

  • Seats used / total — on every org row and at the top of the org page.
  • Expiring licenses — the Overview lists any license ending within 30 days; the org page shows each license's end date.
  • Renewal — create a new license on the same org; learner access continues without interruption.
  • When a license ends — access is removed gently (data kept), never hard-deleted.

5. Finding organizations and accounts

Organizations

Organizations search matches name, contact email, and slug — type at least 3 characters. Click an org to open its page: status, type, tier, slug, public link, address, contacts, seats, licenses, and its audit trail.

Accounts (support)

Accounts finds a learner by email or name and shows their account status, whether deletion is scheduled, and how many purchases they have — enough for “did my purchase go through?” questions.

6. Reports — org detail and the audit feed

Per-organization: open the org — you see seats used/issued, licenses with expiry dates, contacts, and its full audit trail. The aggregate-only engagement dashboard for the customer's own admin lives at /org.

Across everything: the Audit section is the full staff-activity feed — filterable by action, detail, or org name, with Newer/Older paging. This is the record an enterprise or health-plan buyer asks for.

Report elementWhere
Seats / adoptionOverview cards and each org page
License expiryOverview “Expiring soon” + org page
Codes issued / redeemedOrg page
Aggregate engagementOrg dashboard (/org) — cohort ≥5 only
Audit trailPer-org on the org page; global feed under Audit

There is no “export to file” button yet — that's a known gap to raise with engineering rather than something to improvise.

7. Reviewing flags

Flags lists sharing signals raised by the weekly scan — for example, a code pattern suggesting account sharing. Open the account detail, decide as a human, act per the support playbook. A flag is a prompt to review, not an automatic penalty.

8. End-to-end scenarios

Real tasks, start to finish, using only the console.

Scenario 1 — A new senior care facility signs up

“Sunrise Gardens Senior Living wants 25 seats for their residents.”

1
Sign in at mynaani.com/admin.
2
In Organizations, search “Sunrise” first — the org may already exist.
3
Click + New organization: name Sunrise Gardens, type for_profit (senior living), tier, community size.
4
Fill in their street address, city, state, ZIP, phone — and add the activities director as a contact, marked primary.
5
License & go-live: 25 seats, end date a year out, slug sunrise-gardens. Create.
6
Copy the link mynaani.com/for/sunrise-gardens and the 25 codes from the success screen; send them to the contact.

Scenario 2 — A facility adds a second building

1
Create the second org the same way; when the parent-org field is available, link it under Sunrise Gardens.
2
Each site keeps its own license, seats, and codes.
3
The parent's dashboard rolls both sites up automatically.

Scenario 3 — A learner says “my code doesn't work”

1
Open the org — compare codes issued vs seats used.
2
Search the learner's email in Accounts — did they redeem under a different email?
3
If seats are exhausted, raise a bigger license or generate more codes.

Scenario 4 — Renewal season

1
Open Overview — “Expiring soon” lists licenses ending within 30 days.
2
On renewal, attach a new license to the same org — learners keep access.
3
If they don't renew, access de-provisions gently — data kept, not deleted.

Scenario 5 — A flag appears: suspected code sharing

1
Open Flags, read the signal and look up the account in Accounts.
2
Decide as a human — a facility may simply be onboarding a group session. Contact the org's primary contact first.

Scenario 6 — Weekly ops review

1
Overview: pending flags? Seats nearing capacity? Licenses expiring?
2
Flags: clear the queue.
3
Audit: scan the recent feed — every change has a named actor.

9. The privacy boundary — what you can never see

  • You can never see an individual learner's lesson progress, quiz answers, or confidence scores — the boundary is built into the code, not just policy.
  • Engagement numbers are aggregate-only and suppressed entirely under 5 learners.
  • Staff actions are audit-logged with your username. Only open what you need.
  • Never share your login. Password changes go through engineering — rotated as secrets, never sent around.
